# My Forex Demo Trading Journey

*A personal journal of my experiences, lessons, mistakes, and discoveries while learning Forex through demo trading.*

> **Important:** This is a personal learning journal, not financial advice. The purpose of using a demo account is to learn how trading works without risking real money.

## Why I Started Demo Trading

I became curious about Forex trading and wanted to understand what really happens when a trader presses **BUY** or **SELL**.

Instead of immediately putting real money at risk, I decided to learn through a demo account first.

That gave me a chance to explore things that were initially confusing to me:

- currency pairs such as EUR/USD
- BUY and SELL orders
- lot size
- pips
- spread
- Stop Loss
- Take Profit
- leverage
- margin
- market, limit, and stop orders
- floating profit and loss

The more I experimented, the more I realized that Forex is not simply about guessing whether a chart will go up or down.

## My First Demo Experience

My first trades were small in terms of position size. I wanted to see how even a tiny movement in EUR/USD would affect the profit or loss.

One of my early demo trades used **0.01 lot**.

The price moved only a small amount, but the result changed in real time. That was one of the first moments when I understood that the numbers on the chart are connected directly to the position size.

It was a small trade, but it gave me a much bigger lesson.

## Learning About Pips

One of the first things I had to understand was the **pip**.

For most major currency pairs such as EUR/USD, a standard pip is commonly represented by the fourth decimal place.

For example:

`1.15713 → 1.15813`

That is a movement of **10 pips**.

The extra decimal place commonly shown by many brokers is often called a **fractional pip** or **pipette**.

Understanding this made it much easier for me to calculate how far a Stop Loss or Take Profit was from my entry.

## Learning About Lot Size

I also discovered that the same price movement can produce very different results depending on the lot size.

For EUR/USD, a commonly used approximation is:

| Lot size | Approximate pip value |
|---:|---:|
| 0.01 lot | $0.10/pip |
| 0.10 lot | $1.00/pip |
| 1.00 lot | $10.00/pip |

These are useful approximations for learning, although the exact value can vary depending on the account currency and instrument.

This was an important lesson for me:

**Price movement alone does not determine the size of the profit or loss. Position size matters too.**

## The Stop Loss Lesson

I also experimented with Stop Loss.

For example, suppose I SELL EUR/USD at:

`1.15713`

and set the Stop Loss at:

`1.15813`

The difference is 10 pips.

If the position is **0.10 lot**, the approximate loss at that price would be around:

`10 pips × $1/pip = $10`

That simple calculation helped me understand why Stop Loss and lot size should be considered together.

## Margin Finally Started Making Sense

Margin was another concept that confused me at first.

I initially thought that if I had $100 in an account and a trade reserved $20 as margin, then only $80 could be lost.

That is **not** what margin means.

Margin is better understood as money set aside as collateral for an open leveraged position. It does not automatically establish the maximum amount that can be lost.

The potential loss depends mainly on the position size, entry price, exit price, and other trading conditions.

That distinction was a very important part of my learning.

## What I Learned About Leverage

Leverage also sounded intimidating at first.

If an account has leverage such as **500:1**, it means the broker allows a trader to control a much larger position relative to the amount of margin required.

But higher leverage does **not** mean that a trade is automatically safer.

In fact, because leverage allows larger positions, it can also allow losses to accumulate much faster.

So one of the things I am learning is:

> **Leverage changes how much position can be controlled. It does not remove trading risk.**

## Market Orders, Limit Orders and Stop Orders

Another interesting discovery was that BUY and SELL are not the only ways to think about entering a trade.

I learned about:

- **Market Order** — enter at the available market price.
- **Buy Limit** — wait for price to fall to a specified level before buying.
- **Sell Limit** — wait for price to rise to a specified level before selling.
- **Buy Stop** — wait for price to rise to a specified level before buying.
- **Sell Stop** — wait for price to fall to a specified level before selling.
- **Buy Stop Limit / Sell Stop Limit** — combine a stop trigger with a limit order.

At first these names looked complicated.

After experimenting with them, I started seeing the logic behind them: **the trader is basically telling the platform what price condition should happen before entering the trade.**

## The Biggest Lesson So Far

The biggest lesson from my demo experience is that understanding the platform is only the beginning.

Knowing where the BUY button is does not mean I know how to trade profitably.

Knowing pips, lots, leverage and margin does not guarantee a winning trade.

And seeing a trade temporarily move into profit does not mean it will stay there.

Demo trading is giving me something more valuable than a few winning trades:

**the opportunity to make mistakes while learning what those mistakes mean.**

## My Goal

For now, my goal is not to prove that I can make huge profits.

My goal is to understand what I am doing.

I want to be able to look at a trade and understand:

1. Why I entered.
2. How large my position is.
3. How many pips I am risking.
4. How much that risk represents in money.
5. What my Stop Loss means.
6. What my margin requirement means.
7. What could happen if the market moves against me.

If I eventually decide to trade with real money, I want that decision to come **after** learning—not before.
